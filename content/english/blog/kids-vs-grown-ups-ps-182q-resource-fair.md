On September 8, 2026, Food Foundations joined the Child Center of NY at P.S. 182Q in Jamaica, Queens, for its back-to-school resource fair. We set up outside the school with a nutrition challenge built around food pictures and short questions. Our team put the cards on a board and talked with children and caregivers who came over for a look.

## A game for kids and grown-ups

The Child Center invited us to a fair where families could meet neighborhood organizations, watch demonstrations and take home resources. We put together a game called Kids vs. Grown-Ups. A child could start by picking between two pictures. An older student or caregiver looking at the same pictures could use a serving size or a line on the Nutrition Facts label to explain an answer.

Our Pick It cards offered familiar choices like an apple and a banana, or water and soda. We included A and B voting cards so a child who was still learning to read could point or hold up an answer. What mattered was the question. When you pick a snack you like, your own taste settles it. When you pick a drink with less added sugar per serving, there’s a line on the label you can check.

## What a serving tells you

One comparison put plain water beside a can of soda and asked which had more added sugar per serving. Another paired unsweetened and chocolate soy milk. Both soy milk cards used a one-cup serving, so readers could compare the added-sugars line directly. On the cereal cards, the question was about fiber, and each card gave the serving size for that cereal.

One card asked families to work out the amount of added sugar in a whole package. There were two servings in the package, with eight grams of added sugar in each. Eight grams times two gives sixteen grams for the whole package. To try it at home, pick a package in the cupboard and look for two things together. How many servings does it contain, and what amount does the label give for one serving?

## Building a snack with pictures

The snack cards let families mix and match foods such as carrots, hummus, whole-grain crackers, berries and yogurt. Put crackers beside hummus, then swap the crackers for carrots. Which combination would you actually eat? Berries and yogurt give another answer to the same challenge. The cards leave room for a child and a caregiver to choose differently and explain what they like.

Alongside the cards, we shared information about what Food Foundations teaches. At the fair, we also spoke with the Child Center about bringing lessons into classrooms at P.S. 182Q. Thank you to the center for inviting us and to the families who stopped to talk on their back-to-school morning.
